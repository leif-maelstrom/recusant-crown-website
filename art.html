Concept & Process

How the art for Recusant Crown gets made, and where it’s headed.

Where it started

The first Recusant Crown drawings were done in pen on pallet cards during shifts in 2019. Every character on this page began the same way, as a doodle on whatever paper was within reach, long before there was a card game to put them in. The look I’m chasing hasn’t changed since then: sharp, stylish, and a little dangerous.

How I design a character

This has been my process for character design for a long time: practice action and characters, and just draw and redraw until it sticks. The pages below are almost all ideation on Wren, plus the original sketches of Leon Garo.

The card art today

Most of the cards in the game today use AI-generated art, sometimes touched up by hand. As the Wren steps above show, it’s a tool I steer with my sketches and a lot of rewording, and it got playable cards on the table without waiting years for a budget. It isn’t perfect. My angels are the hardest test. They’re meant to be stranger than anything a prompt expects: ivory, hollowed through, more living relic than person with wings. Getting them out of a generator takes a fight every time.

Commissioned art

None of the AI art is meant to be permanent. The plan is to swap it out one card at a time for work by paid human artists, funded by the game itself. The first round is open now: five spoiler cards by outside artists, each drawn in their own style, signed with their name, and shared under the same copyleft license as the rest of the project. Commissioned pieces stay human-made from start to finish; they never pass through an AI tool.

Artists who want to take part can start with the Artist Commission Disclosure.

Down the road

The long-term goal is for Recusant Crown to support me full time. If it gets there, more and more of the finished art will come straight from my own sketchbook, the way the pages above do now.

More sketches and work in progress will be posted here as they’re made.

Free Printables

Planner and journal pages for each faction’s flower. Every set has a weekly planner, a lined journal page, a dotted sketch page and collage paper with cut-out tags, and each page leaves a blank plate for you to write the page number in.

  • Color-lite pages are made for plain white paper and a home printer. Only the flower prints in full color, so they go easy on ink.
  • Full color pages keep the painted backgrounds. They look rich but use a lot of ink, especially Nightshade.
  • US Letter size. Print at 100% (“actual size”). Most home printers leave a thin white edge on the full-color pages.
  • Free to print, share and remix under CC BY-SA 4.0, like the rest of Recusant Crown.
