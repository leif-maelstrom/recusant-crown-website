Concept & Process

How the art for Recusant Crown gets made, and where it’s headed.

Where it started

Recusant Crown began in 2019 as pen drawings on pallet cards at work. It grew on spare copy paper and napkins, with Sharpies and whatever pencil was nearby. It started as a short comic idea inspired by Tetsuya Nomura, Tite Kubo, and other wildly stylish artists, and that look is still the target: anime with teeth.

How I design a character

This has been my process for character design for a long time: practice action and characters, and just draw and redraw until it sticks. The pages below are almost all ideation on Wren, plus the original sketches of Leon Garo.

The card art today

Right now, a lot of the card art is AI-generated, and some of it is refined by hand afterward. It’s cheap and fast, and it let me see the cards and play with them years before I could have afforded anything else. It also has limits. Some of my ideas, my angels especially, are too strange for any prompt to get right.

The Recusant sigil is the exception. I’m drawing that one by hand.

Commissioned art

The AI card art is a placeholder. As soon as this project brings in any money, I want to replace it card by card by paying real artists to make it. That has already started: I’m commissioning five spoiler cards from outside artists, each in the artist’s own style, credited by name, and released copyleft like everything else here. Commissioned pieces are the artists’ own work, and they are never run through AI tools.

If you’re an artist and want in, read the Artist Commission Disclosure first.

Down the road

If Recusant Crown ever earns enough that I can do this full time, I’ll be drawing a lot of it myself. I make comics, and I’d love nothing more than to put the pencil back where it belongs.

More sketches and work in progress will be posted here as they’re made.
