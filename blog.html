October Progress

October has been the most productive month Recusant Crown has had in the seven years I’ve been carrying it around in my head. Here’s what happened.

First, the context. I still work full time in a factory. Recusant Crown happens in the early mornings, an hour or three before the house wakes up, with coffee in one hand and a dog leaning on my leg. Then I go to work, come home, and spend the rest of the day with my wife and kids.

So yes, I’m a blue-collar guy writing about exiled kings, stolen princesses and fairy courts. I spend my days making parts and my mornings making up a multiverse, and only one of those jobs comes with a dental plan. I figure a factory worker designing royal families he’ll never meet is about as recusant as it gets.

The foundation got poured

My big problem has never been ideas. It’s finishing things. I don’t really worldbuild on purpose; I already know this setting in my gut. The trouble is getting it out of my head and onto paper where I can actually use it. This month I finally did that.

  • It has a home. Recusant Crown now lives at recusantcrown.com.
  • It’s officially copyleft. Everything is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. If you want to build on this world, you can, as long as you share what you make the same way. I want this to be a place other people can play in.
  • The lore is written down. The Setting Bible and Plot Bible are now living documents instead of a pile of notes. I went through and closed out around fifty open questions and contradictions, some of them years old: who the Prophet really is, what happened to the vanished princesses, the fortress of Shatterheim and the three siblings who guard it. I’m not spoiling any of that here. But it’s settled, and that means I can write toward it.
  • The card game has rules. The Rules Bible is cleaned up, and a player-facing rulebook is underway. The first set is planned at 250 cards across the four factions plus neutral Essence cards. You can already pick cards on the site and print them on plain paper.
  • Playtesting starts this week. The game has gotten too big to hold in my head without actually playing it, so the first playtesters are my wife and kids. I’ve built a log to track every game. They are going to find every broken card I made, and they’re going to enjoy it.

The story is moving

The lore had gotten way ahead of the writing. This month I pulled it back.

  • Chapter 1, “Berthing,” and Chapter 2, “Disaster,” are finished. Chapter 2’s back half was written on almost no sleep, and it showed. It got a real revision.
  • Chapter 3, “Undertow,” is done. This is the fall of Shin-Seibu: a storm, a road trip and a city coming apart. It’s the best thing I’ve written for this project so far, and it sets up everything that comes next for Nikko and Wren.
  • A new fairy tale is almost done. “The Rose Untouchable” is the hidden story underneath Sleeping Beauty and Beauty and the Beast: the prince who loved the sleeping princess and never said so, the three chances he had to do right, and the Dark One who made him pay for wasting them. It’s a tragedy, told the old way, and it matters a great deal for where Wren’s story goes.

I’ve also mapped out the rest of Arc 1. Each of the five arcs is built to stand on its own, so even if I only ever finished Arc 1, you’d get a complete story. I plan to finish more than Arc 1.

About the art

Let’s be honest about something. Right now, a lot of the card art is AI-generated. It’s cheap, it’s fast, and it let me see the cards and play with them years before I could have afforded anything else. It also has limits. Some of my ideas, my angels especially, are too strange for any prompt to get right.

Here’s the part I’m genuinely excited about: the AI art is a placeholder. As soon as this project brings in any money, I want to start replacing it, card by card, by paying real artists to make it. Every card that sells is a step toward commissioning the next piece. And because this is all copyleft, that art goes back into a world everyone can build on.

And if this goes really well, if Recusant Crown ever earns enough that I can come home from the factory and do this full time, I’ll be drawing a lot of it myself. I make comics. I’m already drawing the Recusant sigil by hand. I’d love nothing more than to put the pencil back where it belongs.

What’s next

  • Family playtests, and a lot of fixing cards based on them.
  • Chapter 4, which opens with Skal.
  • “The Rose Untouchable” goes up on the site.
  • Getting the first card set to alpha, then private alpha tests before the end of the year.

If you’ve read this far, thank you. Every chapter you read and every card you print out on your kitchen printer tells me this is worth getting up early for.

October 2026

Seek the Crown

Hi, and welcome to Recusant Crown. Thanks for stopping by.

This project has been quietly growing for seven years. I've built it in the gaps: between shifts at the factory, between errands and chores, between taking care of my wife and kids. It started on spare copy paper and napkins, with Sharpies and whatever pencil was nearby, and it added up a little at a time. Through all of that, it held its shape. Recently I realized I finally had enough material, and with the tools now available to all of us, enough help, to actually finish it.

So here it is.

Where it came from

Like most of my projects, Recusant Crown started as a short comic idea. I wanted to make something inspired by Tetsuya Nomura, Tite Kubo, and other wildly stylish artists. Then it ballooned, the way unfinished projects do, into a sprawling multiverse: a place to tell new stories, show off characters I love, and, most important to me, give new fairy tales somewhere to live.

We live in a world desperate for fairy tales, but most of us pretend we're too old or too jaded for them. I'm not writing silly stories for children. I'm writing faerie tales: stories of humanity against a world of patterns, stories about stories, and the horror of realizing you're trapped inside one that's carrying you far from where you meant to go.

Stories like that need a frame these days. Many of us were never told them, so we don't know how to tell them to our own kids. I hope you'll read a few of these to yours. For the rest of us, jaded adults and teenagers alike, I've wrapped them in young adult action adventure, because no one with a soul can resist it.

What it is

Recusant Crown is a multimedia project: a trading card game at the center, surrounded by short stories, illustrations, anime-style characters, retold public domain tales, and original faerie tales. All of it shares one world, one set of rules, and one dream.

The core idea is that the multiverse is made of dream. Traveling between worlds is called Dreaming, and the card game takes place on a dreamscape.

The board is the dream.

That's the game in four words. Your creatures aren't just your army; they generate the Clarity that powers your plays, so your economy and your board are the same thing. Your Dream Anchors, the top nine cards of your deck, are set out before the game begins, three per lane. Break through your opponent's anchors, and you win.

It's a lane-based game with three lanes, creatures pushing forward, and one action per turn. It plays like a chess match, quiet and deliberate, each move building on the last, until the lanes clash and it erupts into an anime battle.

Underneath, it's about what it takes to create: losing yourself to an ideal, and sacrificing what you like for what you love. Light and darkness aren't good and evil here. They're forces, and the people who wield them are complicated.

The tone is anime with teeth: stylish and fast, with real weight underneath.

Why it's free

I care deeply about open source and the public domain, and I've never liked how tightly copyright locks creative work away. But I'm a working man, and I can't justify making things purely for free. That would cost my family too much.

So I needed a way to make art that's as free as possible, free enough for a community to build around it, without letting a big company swallow it whole, and without begging for donations or chasing investors.

My answer is copyleft multimedia. Everything on this site is free to use, share, and build on. You can make your own things and sell them, including with art from this site. There's one condition: credit your source loud and proud, and keep whatever you make copyleft too. Take what's here, make it yours, and leave the door open for the next person. Recusant Crown is released under CC BY-SA 4.0.

In return, I ask one thing: when cards and merch become available, buy a box. That keeps the lights on, and it keeps me honest, building a game good enough to stay fun for years.

What's next

The card game is in active development, and I'm running private alphas before the end of the year. I'll report back on how they go, along with design notes, new fiction, and whatever else comes up along the way.

Seek the crown. More soon.
